# Copy-ready itineraries and usable Plan B choices

Apply this format to a finished itinerary or substantive draft, in the user's
language. Keep a short trip compact. Intake, a single recommendation or a small
follow-up does not need the full template.

## Standalone guide structure

Use clear headings and short chronological paragraphs or bullets. Favor text
that remains readable when copied into notes or a chat; use tables only when
they make a comparison or budget easier to read. Do not output internal JSON,
tool transcripts, long research diaries, or references such as “as above” that
make the guide depend on the conversation.

1. **Trip at a glance:** destination and exact dates, starting area, party,
   transport, budget scope, recommended route and the principal tradeoff. Label
   a draft and its assumptions. Do not expose an unnecessary home address.
2. **Route and day plan:** give the ordered route before the timeline. For each
   block show time/range, named destination or navigation target, core or
   removable status, door-to-door approach, activity/queue/meal/rest allocation,
   and any reservation or last-entry condition. Put an applicable Plan B beside
   that block. Separate add-ons from the committed day and explain when to cut
   them. Include the return, exit or turnaround logic.
3. **Meals and lodging:** name researched meal choices when recommending them;
   give location, budget scope and opening/availability status. Reuse the meal
   block instead of repeating the timeline. State whether lodging is useful;
   for overnight trips compare suitable areas/properties and total costs under
   the lodging reference. Do not force hotel listings into a day trip.
4. **Weather and preparation:** identify the relevant date, forecast issue and
   validity period, impact on the plan, useful equipment, and decision/recheck
   time. Say when the trip date is outside the forecast horizon.
5. **Budget:** currency and party scope, category amounts, base total,
   contingency, optional increments and Plan B net changes. Label allowances
   and unknowns. State what is excluded and why.
6. **Choices and final checks:** explain relevant skips; summarize only the
   contingencies worth finding quickly. List unresolved actions with who must
   act, latest decision time and consequence. Add concise linked sources with
   their applicability/checked date, and the actual time/budget audit outcome
   with remaining limits. Never imply an unrun check passed.

Merge overlapping sections when helpful. Sources can sit beside critical facts
and be grouped at the end for convenience; critical conditions must remain
beside the affected activity. Leave out irrelevant modules with a brief reason
when their absence would otherwise confuse the traveler.

## A Plan B must be executable

Plan A is the default. Plan B is a mutually exclusive substitution or exit,
whereas a removable add-on is an optional extra. Normally select two to four
meaningful contingency choices for a short trip, fewer if fewer are useful.
Do not multiply every possible combination.

For each consequential choice provide:

- **Trigger and cutoff:** what the traveler can observe, and when to decide.
  A chosen queue threshold is a planning rule, not a claim about today's queue.
  Derive the latest feasible switch from the next fixed commitment.
- **Specific action:** a named, researched alternative and its location or
  navigation target; or a practical return, postponement or cancellation step.
  Explain which desired experience it preserves and any compromise.
- **Switch logistics:** departure point, travel mode, door-to-door switch time,
  relevant availability/entry conditions and their evidence status. Check that
  it avoids the original bottleneck, such as a shared closure or rain exposure.
- **Cost and schedule effect:** net cost difference for the same party/scope,
  revised time to rejoin, next protected booking, and which optional item drops.
  Use estimates/ranges when warranted. A plan that exceeds the cap or deadline
  is a clearly described tradeoff requiring a choice, not a ready fallback.

Concise presentation in Chinese can follow this pattern, omitting irrelevant
fields rather than filling blanks with invented facts:

> **Plan B｜触发条件与最晚决定时间**：换到【具体地点及位置】。
> 从【当前点】乘【方式】门到门约【时间，注明依据/估算】；
> 【营业/预约核实状态】。比原方案净增/节省【金额及口径】，
> 【时刻】回接【下一项目】；删去【可删项目】以保住【固定安排】。

### Crowded meals

Choose an actual second restaurant within a feasible walk/drive, with a suitable
meal window, cuisine/diet fit and party budget. Research its operating status;
do not assert live seats or queues without evidence. A second branch inside the
same congested complex may share the bottleneck. Include walking, waiting,
ordering, eating and payment in the replacement block, not just map travel.
Do not recommend an unreserved restaurant to solve a fixed booking problem if
it also requires a reservation the traveler cannot obtain.

If no named option can be verified, say so. A supported takeaway option or a
bring-food-in-advance plan may be useful, with a food allowance and place to eat
where supported. Clearly distinguish preparation before departure from a
substitution available on the spot. Do not present “find something nearby” as
an executable backup, or erase the meal to catch up.

### Weather, access, delays and fatigue

Select only relevant disruptions: rain/heat/wind, attraction closure or sold-out
entry, full parking, a missed service, or fatigue. An indoor alternative must
have compatible opening/booking conditions; a shortcut needs a supported exit
and return path. For outdoor routes use the relevant weather/access conditions
and latest turnaround, rather than inventing universal numeric safety limits.
When no suitable alternative exists, an explicit return or postponement with
its transport and cost implications is a valid Plan B.

## Check the selected branch, not two simultaneous trips

Recompute the affected segment through the rejoin point and fixed departure,
including transit, meals and recovery. Recalculate the whole-party total and
reserve. Subtract only avoidable original spending; nonrefundable tickets,
deposits and already-paid parking remain costs. Account for extra transport.

Audit the default and each materially different offered branch independently
under the same original constraints. Do not insert both restaurants into one
clocked schedule or charge both refundable alternatives. State if options can
be combined; otherwise multiple simultaneous disruptions require a fresh check.
An unavailable quote or unverified connection stays conditional, with a latest
check and a practical exit. Offering choices does not execute a reservation,
cancellation or payment.
